# Biomedical Research Notebook

## Research Paper 1: Mayo Clinic (Feuerstein & Cheifetz, 2014)
* **Citation:** Feuerstein, J. D., & Cheifetz, A. S. (2014). Ulcerative colitis: epidemiology, diagnosis, and management. *Mayo Clinic Proceedings*, 89(11), 1553-1563.
* **Findings:** 
    * Mild: <4 stools/day, no systemic toxicity.
    * Moderate: ≥4 stools/day, minimal toxicity.
    * Severe: >6 bloody stools/day + **tachycardia (high HR)**, fever, anemia.
* **Significance to Project:** Directly links elevated heart rate (tachycardia) to systemic inflammatory toxicity during active UC flares, supporting our use of Apple Watch Resting HR as a primary predictive feature.

## Research Paper 2: JAMA Review (Gros & Kaplan, 2023)
* **Citation:** Gros, B., & Kaplan, G. G. (2023). Ulcerative colitis in adults: a review. *JAMA*, 330(10), 951-965.
* **Findings:**
    * Affects over 400 per 100,000 people in North America.
    * 20% hospilization rates within 5 years
    * Life expectancy reduced by approximately 5 years
    * Advanced medical therapy response rate is only 30%–60%, resulting in frequent breakthrough flare-ups.
    * Used Mayo Score (disease activity index) and Montreal Classification (disease location index) as clinical scoring.
* **Significance to Project:**
    * Medications only work 30%–60% of the time, meaning most IBD patients still suffer unpredictable flare-ups.
    * 20% of patients get hospitalized within 5 years of diagnosis.
    * Current tests (colonoscopies and stool samples) are invasive and only happen every few months or years. Our project fills this gap with continuous, daily smartwatch tracking to catch flares early and prevent hospitalizations.
